Phase 1 Authorization (Seconds)

  • Auth Hold: Issuer confirms funds/credit line and places temporary hold.
  • Real-time Checks: CVV match, AVS (Address Verification), 3D-Secure challenge.
  • Decline Types: Soft declines (insufficient funds, retryable) vs Hard declines (stolen card, closed account).

Phase 2 Clearing & Settlement

  • Batch Capture: Merchant batches approved auths at end-of-day.
  • Gross-to-Net: Network routes clearing messages, nets balances between banks.
  • Payout: Acquirer deducts fees and pays net funds into merchant bank account (T+1 to T+3).

Fee Waterfall Economic Cut

  • Interchange (~1.5%–2.5%): Paid directly to Issuer for credit risk & loyalty rewards.
  • Assessment Fee (~0.13%–0.15%): Paid to Visa/Mastercard for rail usage.
  • Acquirer Markup (e.g. 0.10% + $0.10): Paid to processor for gateway & servicing.
Liability & Disputes: Acquirer assumes credit risk if merchant goes bankrupt before fulfillment. Under 3D Secure (EMV 3DS), fraud liability shifts from the merchant to the card issuer.
SECTION 02

Non-Card Payment Method Taxonomy

A2A / Bank Rails

Direct bank transfers bypassing card rails. Lower fee, high consumer trust.

  • Instant Push: UPI (India), Pix (Brazil), Faster Payments (UK). Instant finality.
  • Direct Debit / Pull: ACH (US), SEPA Direct Debit (EU). High return risk (NSF).
  • Economics: Flat low fees (<$0.20 or basis points), no interchange cut.

Delayed / Vouchers

Offline cash vouchers generated online, paid at physical retail locations.

  • Boleto Bancário (Brazil): Barcode ticket paid at bank/lottery shops.
  • OXXO (Mexico): Cash voucher paid at convenience stores.
  • Konbini (Japan): Convenience store cash payment.
  • Latency: Asynchronous confirmation (hours to days). Zero chargeback risk.

Digital Wallets

Container instruments managing credentials and checkout friction.

  • Pass-Through Wallets: Apple Pay, Google Pay. Tokenizes underlying card (DPAN/FPAN).
  • Stored-Value Wallets: PayPal, Alipay, WeChat Pay. Closed-loop balances with merchant ecosystems.
  • Conversion Boost: 1-click biometric authentication lifts mobile checkout.

Crypto / Stablecoins

Public ledger rails providing programmable global settlement.

  • USDC / USDT: Dollar-pegged assets on high-throughput L1/L2s (Solana, Base).
  • Advantages: 24/7/365 settlement, sub-second finality, zero cross-border FX margins.
  • Handoffs: Regulated on/off ramps handle local currency conversion.
SECTION 03

Rules, Compliance & Fraud Architecture

Regulation

  • Durbin Amendment (US): Caps debit interchange for banks >$10B to $0.21 + 0.05%. Exempts fintech credit and small bank debit.
  • IFR (Europe): Caps interchange at 0.20% (debit) and 0.30% (credit).
  • PSD2 / PSD3 & SCA: Mandates multi-factor authentication on electronic European transactions.

Compliance & Data

  • PCI-DSS: Security standard for storing/processing cardholder data. Tokenization (e.g. Stripe Elements) reduces audit scope to SAQ-A.
  • KYC / AML / OFAC: Mandatory customer identification, beneficial ownership checks, and sanction screening.
  • Settlement Holding: Rolling reserves held to protect against sudden chargeback spikes.

Fraud Typologies

  • Card-Not-Present (CNP): Identity theft and stolen card credentials testing.
  • Friendly Fraud: Cardholder falsely claims unrecognized charge or non-delivery.
  • Account Takeover (ATO): Credential stuffing targeting stored wallet payment methods.
  • Card Testing: Bot networks testing thousands of stolen PANs with micro-charges.
SECTION 04

Business Models: Funds Flow Comparison

Subscriptions & SaaS

Recurring Merchant-Initiated Transactions (MIT) following initial Customer-Initiated (CIT) setup.

  • Card Account Updater (CAU): Automated updating of expired card numbers across networks.
  • Smart Dunning: Intelligent retries timed to consumer payday cycles to prevent involuntary churn.
  • Mandatory Auth Flags: Stored credential indicators (credential-on-file) required by Visa/MC.

Marketplaces & Platforms

Tri-party funds flow: Buyer pays platform, platform takes take-rate, seller receives payout.

  • Merchant of Record (MoR): Platform handles all tax, compliance, and dispute liability (e.g. Gumroad).
  • PayFac / Split Payments: Platform routes gross to sub-merchant accounts and claims commission (e.g. Shopify).
  • Money Transmission Licenses (MTL): Holding buyer funds requires escrow compliance or bank partnership.

B2B & Enterprise

High-value invoice matching, structured purchase orders, and Net 30/60 trade credit terms.

  • Level 2 & Level 3 Data: Passing line-item invoice data, tax IDs, and freight codes cuts interchange by 0.50%–1.00%.
  • Virtual Cards: Single-use dynamic PANs generated per PO with exact amount & supplier lock.
  • Reconciliation: Automated matching against ERP systems (NetSuite, SAP).
SECTION 05

Key Takeaways: 10 Core Principles of Global Payments

1. Payments is Risk Pricing: Every basis point in fees reflects who bears fraud, chargeback, or settlement default risk.
2. Interchange Drives Consumer Credit: The 2% card fee isn't network profit—it funds points, cash back, and issuer credit lines.
3. Auth is a Promise, Settlement is Cash: An authorization holds funds; final transfer occurs hours or days later during batch clearing.
4. Asymmetric Underwriting: Issuers underwrite consumers; acquirers underwrite merchants against delivery failure.
5. Local Payment Methods Win Globally: Cards represent under 30% of e-commerce in LatAm and Asia; local rails (Pix, UPI, wallets) dominate conversion.
6. Tokenization Decouples Compliance: Storing network tokens instead of raw PANs shrinks merchant PCI scope while boosting authorization rates.
7. Chargeback Ceilings are Fatal: Crossing 1% dispute-to-transaction ratio triggers card brand fine programs (VDMP/VFMP) and processor termination.
8. MoR vs. PayFac Trade-Off: Being Merchant of Record takes on global sales tax and dispute liability; PayFac pushes liability to sellers.
9. Real-Time Rails Flatten Intermediaries: Instant A2A rails (FedNow, Pix) collapse multi-day settlement windows to seconds with negligible fees.
10. Conversion vs. Fraud Equilibrium: The highest payment revenue comes not from zero fraud, but from eliminating false positive declines on legitimate buyers.
